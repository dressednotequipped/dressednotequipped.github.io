The ManifestoI.

You are not climbing the Watzmann. You are getting a flat white.

Somewhere between the fall of the Berlin Wall and the rise of the all-day fleece, the European man over fifty made a quiet, terrible bargain. He swapped his overcoat for a softshell. He swapped his shoes for footwear that bills itself as technical. He took to the cobblestones of Bamberg and Bruges in the apparel of a man preparing to bivouac.

This gazette is not a moral panic. It is an intervention, written in the spirit of friendship. We have nothing against mountains. We have nothing against Jack Wolfskin in its proper context, which is, broadly, a mountain. We do, however, have an objection to walking past a respectable trattoria and seeing a man in his mid-fifties dressed for a weather event that is not happening.

You are an adult. You have a job, or you had one. You have books. You have opinions about wine. The clothing should match the man.

Part Two

The Six Crimes

An incomplete dossier of offences committed daily on the streets of Munich, Hamburg, and indeed Coburg.

№ 01

The Commute Anorak

A Goretex shell, fully sealed, hood up, on a 4°C morning in a tram carriage that is internally tropical. He arrives at the office sweating like a man who has just summited the Hörnli Ridge. He has, in fact, summited the U-Bahn.

Verdict — Indefensible
№ 02

The Trail Runner At Dinner

Salomon Speedcross, 4mm lugs caked in mud from no terrain in particular, paired with a clean shirt and chinos. The shoe is a brown-and-orange neon scream beneath an otherwise civilised man. The waiter looks down. The waiter understands.

Verdict — Aggravated
№ 03

The Technical Rucksack

32 litres. Hip belt. Hydration sleeve. Daisy-chain webbing for ice axe attachment. Inside: a paperback, a banana, and the keys to a Volvo. He is not crossing the Karwendel. He is collecting his daughter from ballet.

Verdict — Egregious
№ 04

The Zip-Off Trouser

A garment that is, optimistically, two garments. Both worse than one. The horizontal zip line sits across the thigh like a scar from an unsuccessful operation. He wears them to a wine bar. He has not, since 2003, removed the lower portion.

Verdict — Capital
№ 05

The Fleece Gilet Over The Shirt

The international uniform of the man who has confused a pension review with a hunting trip. The shoulders bunch. The pockets bulge with car keys and a Leatherman he has never used. He believes this looks practical. It looks like a pyjama waistcoat over a school uniform.

Verdict — Pitiable
№ 06

The Convertible Watch Band

A Garmin the size of a small saucepan, on a velcro strap, paired with a blazer at a wedding. He glances at it during the speeches. He is monitoring his resting heart rate. The bride notices. The bride is no longer thinking about the bride.

Verdict — Sentenced

The PrinciplesIII.

Four truths before you spend a euro.

i.

Fit matters more than brand.

A €40 shirt that fits beats a €400 one that does not. Tailored cuts. Not tight. Tight is for men who are still trying to prove something.

ii.

Buy fewer things, better.

One pair of proper Goodyear-welted shoes will outlive three pairs of trainers and look better at dinner. The maths is on your side.

iii.

If it has a logo, ask why.

A small woven label inside is craftsmanship. A large embroidered logo on the chest is paid advertising you have funded yourself.

iv.

The good brands sell through proper retailers.

Italian houses controlling their distribution is a sign of quality, not inconvenience. Find Breuninger. Find Mr Porter. Find Farfetch.

A Note From The Editor

This is what I actually wear, more or less.

Everything on the previous list is a brand I own, have owned, have handled in a German shop, or have queued up in a browser tab and reasoned myself out of buying for the third time this year. None of these are paid placements. There are no affiliate kickbacks at the time of writing, though if that ever changes you will see it stated, plainly, at the top of any page where it applies. Werbung means advertising. I will use the word.

The list is also incomplete and personal. It reflects a 56-year-old Englishman living in Coburg, with a frame that suits Italian trousers, a fondness for British shoes, a sober view of Scottish knitwear, and the tired conviction that almost nobody on the streets of mainland Europe needs a Goretex shell to attend a dental appointment.

If you disagree, send a properly composed letter. If you agree, get the Incotex.

Craig Kinnell Editor & Sole Correspondent
