A Supplement for the Sporting Gentleman

Eighteen Holes,
& No Excuses

For the man who has spent the summer on the course and returned to civilisation with one tanned hand, a laser in a holster, and trousers designed by a sportswear firm for a body he does not have.

By Craig Kinnell, who plays
No Rangefinder Inside Ten Metres ◆ One Logo, Maximum ◆ Hem Touches The Shoe ◆ Sun Cream On Both Hands
The ConfessionI.

The editor has been away. He was on the course.

A man who writes a gazette against technical clothing should not, in fairness, spend an entire summer in a sport that has turned technical clothing into a religion. He did. This supplement is the field report, filed from the inside, with the guilt of a participant.

Golf is the last refuge of the equipped man. Nowhere else will a grown adult wear a moisture-wicking polo to walk six kilometres at the pace of a museum visit, carry a laser to measure a putt, and apply sun cream to precisely one hand. The game is beautiful. What happens around it needs a word.

Nothing below is aimed at anyone in particular. Everything below is aimed at most of us.

Part Two

The Course Crimes

Offences observed between the first tee and the nineteenth hole, April to October.

№ 01

The Five-Metre Survey

Green 7. A €400 laser rangefinder leaves its magnetic holster to measure a putt he could cover by lying down twice. Slope mode on. Target locked. The target is a hole. It is not going anywhere. "Five point two," he announces. He three-putts.

Verdict — Aggravated
№ 02

The One-Glove Problem

The only garment in menswear deliberately sold as half a pair. One hand gets the Mediterranean. The other spends six months in leather and emerges each October like a hand in witness protection. The dermatologist asks if it is spreading. Only between April and October.

Verdict — Chronic
№ 03

Performance Fabric For A Man Who Walks

Four-way stretch. Moisture management. UV protection factor fifty. Ventilation zones mapped to the body's heat signature. Deployed to stroll six kilometres and sit in a cart for most of them. The polo is training for a marathon. The man is training for lunch.

Verdict — Overruled
№ 04

Clubhouse Camouflage

Quarter-zip, half-zip, soft-shell gilet over the quarter-zip. Every man in the bar dressed as a pharmaceutical representative on the second day of a team-building retreat. You cannot tell the members from the sales conference. Neither can they.

Verdict — Indistinguishable
№ 05

The Logo Audit

Cap, polo, belt, glove, shoes, bag, umbrella, head covers. Count them. The record at our club stands at eleven separate brands on one man, two of them competitors. He is not a golfer. He is a hoarding at a motorway services.

Verdict — Egregious
№ 06

Dress Code Versus Dressed

No denim. No collarless shirts. No trainers in the clubhouse. Pink trousers printed with tiny whales: permitted. Plaid in four colours that do not occur in nature: encouraged. The rules were written to keep standards up. They kept the jeans out and let everything else in.

Verdict — Contempt
Field SpecimensII½.

The evidence, from the eighteenth green.

Reconstructions, not photographs of real members. Any resemblance to the man in your four-ball is regrettable and probably accurate.

The equipment knew exactly where the hole was. The man still has no idea.
— Overheard, Green 7
Dressed InsteadIII.

Four rules for the course.

i.

A plain polo, in navy.

No mesh panels, no thermoregulation zones, no chest logo the size of a beer mat. A collar that stays flat. It should work at the bar afterwards without anyone asking how your round went.

ii.

Trousers that fit.

Slim, plain-front, grey or stone. The hem just touches the shoe. Then €15 at a tailor, which beats any stretch technology yet invented.

iii.

One belt. Leather. Brown.

No ratchet mechanism. No holster for your equipment. A belt holds up trousers. That is the entire job description.

iv.

Use your eyes on the green.

Keep the rangefinder for the 160-metre approach. Inside ten metres, look at the hole. Your eyes came free, and they have slope mode.

A Note From The Editor

I own a rangefinder. I am not proud.

In the interest of full disclosure: I carry one. I have used it on a green. I have a glove line that would interest a dermatologist, and at least one polo with a ventilation zone. This supplement is not written from the moral high ground. It is written from the left rough.

The season is ending in Franconia. The clubs are going into the cellar. If you spend the winter doing one thing, take your golf trousers to a tailor. Come April, you will look like a man who plays golf, rather than a man who has been issued it.

Craig Kinnell Editor & Sole Correspondent, Handicap Undisclosed
